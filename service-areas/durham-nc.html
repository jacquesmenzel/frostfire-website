Durham is a heating town for us

In most of the places we work, summer and winter calls run close to even. Durham is different. 57% of our Durham jobs fell between November and March, and only 19% between June and September. January (27 jobs) and December (21) are our two busiest Durham months by a wide margin.

The job notes match the calendar. "No heat" was the complaint on at least 47 Durham jobs, more than double the 18 "no cooling" calls. Census estimates show about 59% of occupied Durham homes heat with electricity, mainly heat pumps, and 39% with natural gas. So a winter call here could be a gas furnace that won't light or a heat pump stuck on emergency heat.

The equipment is younger than you'd think

Durham's housing is not new. The Census puts the median Durham home at 1995, with about 30% built before 1980. The equipment inside those homes is a different story. Of the 10 Durham units we could date from the nameplate, the median was 7.5 years old at the visit, and every one was made between 2013 and 2020. Brands seen: Lennox (6), Goodman (5), Carrier (4), and one each of Amana, Trane, Rheem, Nordyne and International Comfort Products.

That matters for the repair-or-replace question. A 7- or 8-year-old system with a bad capacitor or a wiring fault is a repair, not a sales pitch. Out of 132 Durham jobs, we did one replacement and one estimate. The other 130 were repairs, diagnostics and maintenance.

Durham permits and inspections

Durham handles permits differently from the Wake County towns we serve. Building permits for both the City and County of Durham come from one office, the Durham City-County Building & Safety Department (renamed from City-County Inspections on July 1, 2025). A few things homeowners notice:

  • Permits and inspections run through the Land Development Office portal. Contractors can't self-register; the department sets up the account.
  • The department doesn't take inspection appointments by phone or email, and it doesn't accept paper checks.
  • A residential mechanical job gets a rough-in inspection (after the equipment is set, before ducts are covered) and a final, plus a duct leakage test when the energy code calls for one.

We haven't confirmed how permits work for the parts of the City of Durham that sit in Wake or Orange County. If you're in one of those areas, we check before we start.

Durham FAQ

Who issues HVAC permits in Durham?

The Durham City-County Building & Safety Department, which was called City-County Inspections until July 1, 2025. It covers both the City and County of Durham. Permits and inspections go through its Land Development Office portal, and the contractor needs an account to pull them.

How far is Durham from you?

About 57 minutes and 42 miles from our Clayton shop with no traffic, which makes Durham one of the longer drives we make. We say so up front and give you a real arrival window when you call.

Which Duke Energy company serves my Durham home?

Sources disagree. Duke's own release puts Durham in Duke Energy Carolinas territory, while Durham's economic development office names Duke Energy Progress. The name on your bill is the reliable answer.

Do you work on Sundays in Durham?

Yes, at the same price as a weekday. Nine of our Durham jobs were on a Sunday and 14 on a Saturday.
