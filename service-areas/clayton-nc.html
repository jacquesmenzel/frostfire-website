A Clayton mailing address doesn't mean the Town of Clayton issues your permit

This is the single most useful thing we can tell a Clayton homeowner about HVAC work. Of the 43 completed Clayton jobs we could match to a county parcel record, 29 were in unincorporated Johnston County, 7 were inside town limits and 7 were in the town's extraterritorial jurisdiction (ETJ). The county's own records show the same shape: of 26,116 residential parcels with a Clayton mailing city, 61% are unincorporated, 22% are inside town limits and 16% are in the ETJ.

Who signs off on a replacement depends on which of those three your house sits in:

  • Inside town limits: Town of Clayton Engineering & Inspections (Town Hall, 111 E. 2nd St; inspections 919-553-5002). The town FAQ says a mechanical permit is needed to install, extend, relocate, replace or do general repair on heating, air-conditioning or venting systems. Contractors file through the town's online permit system, and online inspection requests get priority; a voicemail on the inspection line (919-359-8717) by 4 pm gets next-business-day scheduling.
  • Unincorporated county: Johnston County Building Inspections at the Land Use Center, 309 E. Market St, Smithfield (919-989-5060). The county lists HVAC change-outs as permitted work. Its FAQ says applications can't be filed online yet; a simple trade permit can be emailed to inspections@johnstonnc.gov.
  • Clayton ETJ: the town handles planning and zoning there, and the county's inspections page says property outside the town boundary is permitted by the county. Because the ETJ is the in-between case, we check the parcel with the right office before we file.

For you, the practical difference is timing and who shows up for the final inspection. We look the parcel up before quoting a replacement so the permit goes to the right office the first time.

In Clayton, our busy season is winter

55% of our Clayton jobs land between November and March, against 22% in June through September. January alone accounts for 13 of them, and "no heat" is the most common complaint we hear here (10 jobs). Six of the Clayton jobs were on a Sunday.

When you call about no heat, tell us whether you have gas. Clayton is genuinely mixed:

  • The Johnston County assessor lists 62% of Clayton-mailing homes with a heat pump and 36% with ducted forced air, which usually means a furnace.
  • Census estimates for the town itself show 68% of occupied homes heating with electricity and 28% with utility gas.
  • Among the homes we've serviced, the assessor lists 24 as ducted forced air and 18 as heat pumps.

The housing is mostly from the last 30 years: the county's median build year for Clayton-mailing homes is 2005, with the middle 80% built between 1986 and 2021. Inside town limits, the Census puts 37% of homes at 2010 or newer and about 10% before 1980.

Clayton HVAC questions

My address says Clayton. Does the town issue my HVAC permit?

Only if the property is inside town limits. Most Clayton-address homes we have serviced were in unincorporated Johnston County, where Johnston County Building Inspections issues the mechanical permit. We check the parcel before filing.

Do I need a permit just to replace my outdoor unit in Clayton?

Yes. The Town of Clayton requires a mechanical permit for replacement and general repair of heating, air-conditioning or venting systems, and Johnston County lists HVAC change-outs as permitted work. We pull it as part of the job.

Who supplies electricity and gas in Clayton?

Inside the town's electric system, the Town of Clayton is the provider; it has run public power since 1913. Piedmont Natural Gas serves the Clayton area. For homes outside the town system, check your bill, because we have not confirmed the provider for every address.

Do you charge extra for a Sunday call in Clayton?

No. A Sunday visit costs the same as a weekday one.
