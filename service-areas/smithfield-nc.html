Two kinds of Smithfield housing

Inside town limits, Smithfield is old by Triangle standards. Census estimates put the town's median build year at 1973, with 65% of homes built before 1980 and only 8% built since 2010. The Smithfield houses we've worked in match that: the nine we matched to county records have a median build year of 1975, and the oldest dates to 1908. Eight of those nine are inside town limits.

Widen the lens to every home with a Smithfield mailing address (9,793 residential parcels, including rural ones) and the picture shifts. The median build year there is 1998, about 13% were built before 1950, and 2,312 were built in the 2020s alone. So a Smithfield address can mean a 1920s house near downtown or a two-year-old heat pump in a new subdivision, and we plan the visit differently for each.

In houses built before central air was common, the ductwork was added later. When rooms in an older Smithfield home won't hold temperature, duct routing and sizing are among the first things we check, before we blame the equipment.

All 11 completed Smithfield jobs were repairs or diagnostics. The county assessor lists five of the homes as heat pumps and four as ducted forced air. One job needed a filter drier replaced. Most of these records carry only a short note, so we're not going to describe fixes we can't document.

About that defrost problem: in heating mode, a heat pump's outdoor coil collects frost, and the system briefly reverses to melt it off. If the reversing valve or the defrost control fails, the outdoor coil stays iced and the heat coming out of the vents drops off. If your outdoor unit is a block of ice in January, turn the system to emergency heat if it has it, and call.

Permits: the county office is in town, but you start with the town

Smithfield doesn't have its own inspections department. For work inside town limits, the owner first gets a zoning / land-use permit from the Town of Smithfield, and then Johnston County Building Inspections issues the building or trade permit. The county office is right here at the Land Use Center, 309 E. Market St (919-989-5060).

  • The county lists HVAC change-outs among the projects that need a permit.
  • Its Forms page has a Residential Mechanical Permit Application in fillable and print-only versions.
  • The county FAQ says permits can't be applied for online yet; a simple trade permit can be emailed to inspections@johnstonnc.gov.
  • Since April 1, 2026, construction drawings must be submitted digitally. That applies to drawings, not to a simple trade permit for a system swap.

Smithfield utilities and heating fuel

Electric: inside town, the Town of Smithfield Public Utilities department runs the electric system (an NCEMPA / ElectriCities member). For Smithfield mailing addresses outside the town system, we haven't confirmed the provider, so check your bill.

Gas: Piedmont Natural Gas. Johnston County's economic development office says laterals off Piedmont's main serve Smithfield.

Propane matters here. Census estimates show 64% of Smithfield homes heating with electricity, 26% with utility gas, 8% with propane and about 1% with fuel oil. A propane furnace is set up differently from a natural-gas one, so tell us which you have when you call. County-wide, the assessor lists 65% of Smithfield-mailing homes with a heat pump and 30% with ducted forced air.

Questions from Smithfield homeowners

Do I need both a town permit and a county permit to replace my system in Smithfield?

Inside town limits, the Town of Smithfield handles zoning and land use first, then Johnston County Building Inspections issues the mechanical permit and does the inspection. Smithfield has no inspections department of its own.

Who provides electric and gas service in Smithfield?

Inside town, Town of Smithfield Public Utilities runs the electric system, and Piedmont Natural Gas serves the town. For a Smithfield mailing address outside the town electric system we have not confirmed the provider, so check your bill.

My heat pump's outdoor unit is iced over and the house won't stay warm. What's wrong?

Usually the defrost cycle isn't working, from a failed reversing valve or defrost control. We found a reversing valve and defrost problem in a 1975-built Smithfield home in January 2026. Switch to emergency heat if you have it and call us.

How long does it take you to get to Smithfield?

About 23 minutes, or 12.4 miles, from our base in Clayton without traffic.
