More Cary homes burn gas than not

Census estimates show 55% of occupied Cary homes heat with natural gas and 43% with electricity. That's the reverse of Raleigh and Durham. A Cary winter call is more often a gas furnace: a dirty flame sensor, a bad igniter, an inducer motor or a pressure switch. If you smell gas, leave the house and call Enbridge Gas at (877) 776-2427 first.

Cary's permit process is online-only

The Town of Cary's Inspections & Permits office issues HVAC replacement and duct replacement permits online as administrative trade permits, with no plan review. The rules cover detached homes, townhomes and duplexes, and condos only when each unit has its own system. Paper isn't accepted for these. Repairs like the ones above don't need you to do anything. On a replacement, the licensed contractor files the permit and meets the inspector. The town's help line is 311 or 919-469-4000.

Part of Cary sits in Chatham County, and we haven't confirmed the utilities there.

Questions from Cary homeowners

Does replacing an HVAC system in Cary need a permit?

Yes. The Town of Cary treats residential HVAC replacement and duct replacement as an online administrative trade permit with no plan review. It applies to single-family homes, townhomes and duplexes, and to condos only when the system is self-contained in the unit. Paper applications aren't accepted.

My Cary system is about 18 years old. Repair or replace?

It depends on what failed. A capacitor, drain line or blower motor on an older system is often still worth fixing; a failed compressor or a major refrigerant leak on an 18-year-old system usually isn't. We show you the reading and the options before any work starts.

Who supplies electricity and gas in Cary?

The Town lists Duke Energy for electric and Enbridge Gas (formerly Dominion Energy / PSNC) for natural gas. Town of Apex Electric also serves parts of Cary; we haven't confirmed which neighborhoods.
