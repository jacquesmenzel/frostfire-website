Young systems have their own problems

With nearly half the houses built since 2010, many Sims systems are original builder equipment, some now around 15 years old. The things we'd look at first on that kind of system:

  • Warranty status. Many manufacturer parts warranties depend on registration within a set window after install. Find the model and serial number before you call, and we'll check what's still covered.
  • Refrigerant type. Systems from 2010 to about 2024 almost all use R-410A. Under EPA rules, new systems are moving to lower-impact refrigerants such as R-454B and R-32. That affects repair cost and parts availability. We tell you which you have.
  • Builder-grade install details. Undersized returns, a condensate line with no trap or float switch, a thermostat wired for the minimum. These cause recurring calls that look like equipment failure.
  • Heat pump backup strips. In an all-electric house, the strips carry the load when the heat pump falls behind. A jump in the winter bill is often the first sign. See aux vs. emergency heat.

For ages and replacement timing, see how long an AC lasts in North Carolina.

Wilson County permit steps

Sims is in Wilson County, not Johnston, so the steps differ from most of the towns on our list:

  1. Zoning from the town. Wilson County's planning division says a zoning permit has to come before a building permit, and that parcels in a town's limits or ETJ get zoning from that town.
  2. County building permit. Wilson County's online permit portal tells Sims applicants to upload their zoning documents with the county Building Permit Application.
  3. Inspection. Inspections are scheduled through the county's online portal. Contact: Wilson County Inspections Division, 252-399-2965 option 2, inspections@wilsoncountync.gov.

We confirm the current routing with the county before every replacement in Sims, since this is a county we don't work in every week.
