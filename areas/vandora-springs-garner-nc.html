Basements, split-levels and houses without AC

The older Vandora Springs Rd homes are unusual for this part of Wake County. Of the 125 parcels on the road, 10 are coded with a full basement and 16 with a partial basement. Ranch and split-level designs are common. In most of the newer Garner subdivisions, almost nothing has a basement.

That changes the job. In a basement or split-level house, the furnace or air handler often sits in the basement, with supply runs going up through the floors. That is where we look for rusted heat exchangers on older gas furnaces, condensate lines that drain to a pump instead of by gravity, and rooms on the top level that never cool because the return air path is too small. The county also codes four homes on the road with no central air conditioning at all. If you own one of those and want to add AC, the basement usually makes the duct routing simpler than in a slab ranch.

Older homes in the area are also where we still find add-on systems: a gas furnace for heat with a separate outdoor AC condenser. When those reach the end, the decision is whether to keep gas heat or switch to a heat pump. We will walk you through both before quoting. See furnace installation, AC installation and ductwork.

Permits

The Vandora-named subdivisions are inside Garner town limits, and of the 125 Vandora Springs Rd parcels, 115 are in town and 10 are in Garner's ETJ. Either way the Town of Garner Inspections Department is the permit office. Applications go through the town's SmartGov portal only. We file the mechanical permit for any system replacement and schedule the inspection.

Gas and electric

Garner lists Duke Energy as its electric utility. Natural gas is Enbridge Gas North Carolina, formerly PSNC (some town pages still use the old name). Across Garner's town limits, the Census estimates that about 36% of homes heat with utility gas and 60% with electricity, a higher gas share than ZIP 27529 as a whole (about 25%). Whether gas reaches your specific street in Vandora Springs is not confirmed, so check before planning a gas furnace replacement.

Vandora Springs FAQ

My Vandora Springs house has a basement. Where is my furnace?

In most basement and split-level homes the furnace or air handler is in the basement. About 26 of the 125 homes on Vandora Springs Rd are coded with a full or partial basement.

Can you add central air to an older home without AC?

Yes. Wake County codes four homes on Vandora Springs Rd with no air conditioning. We size the system, plan the duct routing and pull the Town of Garner mechanical permit.

Who issues HVAC permits in Vandora Springs?

The Town of Garner, through its SmartGov portal. That covers both the parcels inside town limits and the ones in Garner's ETJ.

How far are you from Vandora Springs?

About 23 minutes and 12 miles from our Clayton base without traffic.
