How Kenly heats: more propane and furnaces than most of the county

The Johnston County assessor lists heating type for the 2,763 homes with a Kenly mailing address: 57% heat pump, 34% ducted forced air (a furnace), 6% forced air without ducts, 2% none and under 1% baseboard. Many Johnston towns run 65–80% heat pump. Kenly has far more furnaces than that.

The Census (American Community Survey, 2024 5-year) shows what those furnaces burn:

In practice, a furnace call inside Kenly may be natural gas, while a call out in the 27542 countryside is more likely propane. Before we arrive, it helps to know which fuel you have and whether the tank was recently filled. An empty propane tank is a common cause of a no-heat call. See heating repair for what we fix.

We have not confirmed which gas utility serves Kenly addresses. The Census count of piped-gas homes in town tells us service exists, but we don't have a provider map, so we leave the name out.

House age: an old core, newer houses out in the county

  • In town: the Census puts the median Kenly home at about 1973, with roughly two-thirds built before 1980.
  • The whole mailing area: county parcels give a median year of 1992. Since 2020, 485 new homes have been built, the most of any decade on record.
  • Location: 72% of Kenly-addressed homes are in unincorporated county, 20% inside town limits and 8% in Kenly's ETJ.

Pre-1980 houses tend to have duct systems added after the fact and older wiring at the air handler. Newer houses tend to be all-electric heat pump builds. One page can't cover both, so we ask what year your house was built when you call.

Manufactured homes

The county counts 386 double-wides and 33 single-wides with Kenly addresses, and the Census puts manufactured homes at about 27% of housing units in the ZIP. Furnaces and air handlers in these homes must be listed for manufactured-home use, and the under-floor duct is part of nearly every airflow complaint we check.

Permits and power

Permits. Kenly has no inspections department of its own. Johnston County lists it among the towns that use Johnston County Building Inspections (309 E. Market St, Smithfield, 919-989-5060), and the town's zoning/land-use permit comes first. HVAC change-outs are on the county's permit-required list. Kenly also reaches into Wilson County; we haven't confirmed the permit route for those addresses, so we check before quoting a job there.

Electricity. The Town of Kenly utilities page says electric service is not connected to the town and names Progress Energy, the old name for Duke Energy Progress. One third-party guide says Tri-County EMC serves parts of the area. We haven't confirmed that, so check your bill.

Kenly FAQ

Have you worked in Kenly before?

Yes, once so far: a repair visit in April 2026 at a mid-1990s home inside town limits that the county lists with a ducted forced-air system. That is our only completed Kenly job on record, so we won't claim more.

Who issues HVAC permits in Kenly?

For the Johnston County side, Johnston County Building Inspections issues the mechanical permit after the town signs off on zoning. Part of Kenly is in Wilson County, and we have not confirmed the permit route for those addresses. We check each job.

Is Kenly's power from the town?

No. The Town of Kenly says electricity is not connected to the town. Its page still says Progress Energy, which is now Duke Energy Progress.

Do you service propane furnaces around Kenly?

Yes. The Census estimates about 1 in 5 homes in the 27542 ZIP heat with propane, one of the higher shares in our area.
