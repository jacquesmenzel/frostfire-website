The flood-zone rule for HVAC equipment

This is the reason a river page needs to exist. Inside Clayton, the town's flood damage prevention ordinance (2018 amendment) says new or substantially improved HVAC equipment and ductwork in a Special Flood Hazard Area must be at or above the base flood elevation plus 2 feet, or be flood-proofed. A like-for-like maintenance replacement can stay where the old one was.

In practice:

  • If you're swapping a failed condenser for a similar one in the same spot, the rule usually doesn't force a move.
  • If you're adding a system, relocating equipment, or doing a larger renovation, the outdoor unit may need a raised stand or platform, and ductwork in a low crawlspace may need a different route.
  • We don't know which specific homes sit in the flood zone. That's an address-by-address check on the state's flood map, and we do it before we quote.

Outside town limits, Johnston County has its own flood rules. We haven't pulled the county's exact HVAC wording, so we treat any county lot in a mapped flood zone the same careful way and confirm with the county inspector.

Questions from homes near the Neuse

Does my new outdoor unit have to be raised because I live near the river?

Only if your lot is in a Special Flood Hazard Area and the work counts as new or a substantial improvement. In Clayton the equipment must then be at least 2 feet above base flood elevation, or flood-proofed. Like-for-like maintenance replacements can stay put.

How do I know if my house is in the flood zone?

It's an address-by-address check on the official flood map. We look it up before quoting a replacement for any home near the Neuse.

Who issues the HVAC permit for a home near the Neuse?

It depends on the lot: Johnston County for unincorporated land (most river homes), the Town of Clayton inside town limits or the ETJ, and Wake County on the Wake side of Pritchard Road.

No. Sundays cost the same as weekdays. Our July 2026 Neuse Haven repair was on a Sunday.
