Two different Princetons: in town and the countryside

Johnston County records list 2,386 homes with a Princeton mailing address. Only about one in five is inside town limits:

The two groups are different housing stock. The Census puts the median home inside Princeton at about 1977, with 57% built before 1980. Across the whole mailing area, the county's median is 1997, and the 2020s have already added 420 homes, more than any earlier decade.

That last row matters for permits. Johnston County Building Inspections issues HVAC permits for both towns, but the zoning/land-use step goes to whichever town has jurisdiction, so some "Princeton" addresses start at Pine Level Town Hall.

How the houses heat

The county assessor lists 65% heat pump and 29% ducted forced air (a furnace) for Princeton-addressed homes, with about 5% forced air without ducts. That is a higher furnace share than Archer Lodge (18%). The Census fuel mix:

  • In town: 79% electric, 17% propane, 4% utility gas
  • 27569 ZIP: 76% electric, 18% propane, about 5% utility gas

About one in six homes heats with propane, so we get furnace calls from Princeton that we don't get from all-electric towns. Before a burner repair, we confirm the tank has fuel and the supply valve is open. An empty tank causes a lot of "broken furnace" calls. Gas utility service in Princeton is not confirmed beyond the small Census count, so ask us about your street if you're considering a gas conversion.

Manufactured homes: about a quarter of the ZIP

The county counts 315 double-wides with Princeton addresses, and the Census estimates manufactured homes at about 26% of housing units in the 27569 ZIP. For these homes:

  • Furnaces and air handlers must be listed for manufactured-home use, so replacement choices are narrower.
  • The duct runs under the floor inside the belly wrap. A torn wrap or loose crossover duct can dump conditioned air under the house, and it looks like a weak AC.
  • Outdoor units sit on pads that can settle on soft ground. A tilted condenser stresses the refrigerant lines.

Older homes in town

For the pre-1980 houses inside Princeton, the issues are older ducts sized for heat only, return air too small for central AC, and old wiring at the air handler. A duct check often explains a back bedroom that never cools before anyone has to touch the equipment. If you're weighing a new system, our heat pump vs. gas furnace article covers the trade-offs for a propane house too.

Permits and power

Permits. Princeton has no inspections department. Johnston County Building Inspections (Land Use Center, 309 E. Market St, Smithfield; 919-989-5060) issues mechanical permits for HVAC change-outs after the town's zoning/land-use permit. The county doesn't take online applications yet; simple trade permits can be emailed.

Electric. The Town of Princeton FAQ says Duke Energy provides electricity for the area (1-800-452-2777).

Princeton, NC: common questions

Who is the power company in Princeton?

The Town of Princeton FAQ says Duke Energy provides electricity for the area. We have not confirmed whether any electric co-op serves outlying 27569 addresses.

My address says Princeton but I'm near Pine Level. Does that change anything?

Possibly. County records place about 4% of Princeton-mailing homes inside Pine Level town limits. Zoning for those comes from Pine Level, though Johnston County still issues the mechanical permit.

Do you work on propane furnaces in Princeton?

Yes. The Census estimates about 17% of homes in town and 18% across the ZIP heat with propane.

Have you done jobs in Princeton?

Not yet. Our records show no completed jobs in Princeton so far. We do serve the area.
