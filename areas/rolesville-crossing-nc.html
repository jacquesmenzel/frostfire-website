Your system probably uses a new kind of refrigerant

Under EPA's AIM Act rules, residential central air conditioners and heat pumps manufactured since 2025 have moved to lower-global-warming refrigerants, usually R-454B or R-32 instead of the R-410A used for the past fifteen years. These newer refrigerants are rated A2L (mildly flammable), so service needs gauges, recovery machines and leak detectors approved for them. A system installed in a 2026 home very likely uses one of them, and the nameplate on the outdoor unit will say which. When anyone comes to service it, ask whether their tools are rated for A2L.

A first-year checklist for a brand-new system

  1. Find the warranty paperwork. Lennar's warranty terms are in your closing documents. While a builder warranty covers the install, a defect is usually the builder's to fix, and paying someone else first can complicate that claim.
  2. Register the equipment. Many manufacturers give a longer parts warranty only if the unit is registered within a set window after installation. Check the brand's website with the serial number from the outdoor unit.
  3. Learn your filter size and location. New-construction dust, from drywall sanding and trim work, loads filters quickly in the first months. Check it monthly at first.
  4. Watch the condensate line in the first summer. Construction debris can clog a new drain line. Water near the indoor unit, or a float switch that shuts the system off, is the usual sign.
  5. Note whether it's gas or electric heat. We haven't confirmed whether gas is available here. A flue pipe means a gas furnace. An outdoor unit that runs in winter means a heat pump.

Townhome or single-family changes the service visit

In the Designer townhomes, the indoor unit and the outdoor condensers are usually close to the neighbors' units. Before you need service, find out which outdoor unit is yours and where your shutoff is. In the Summit and Highland houses, there may be more than one system for upstairs and downstairs. If one floor is fine and the other isn't, tell us that when you call. It narrows the problem quickly.
