Granite Falls was built in three waves

Wake County's October 2026 real estate extract lists 186 homes in Granite Falls, all single-family. The first section (Phase 1A) dates to 2007. Phase 2 went up from 2013 to 2018, and Phases 5 and 6 from 2018 to 2022. ICG Homes builds in The Peak at Granite Falls section. The median home here was built in 2018, so for most owners the HVAC system is still the original one.

Each wave sits at a different point in an HVAC system's life:

  • 2007 to 2009 homes (15 of them). The original equipment is now 17 to 19 years old. Units built before 2010 may run on R-22, a refrigerant no longer produced, so a leak repair on one of those can cost more than it's worth. On these we put repair and replacement prices side by side before you decide.
  • 2010s homes (105). Mid-life systems. This is when run capacitors, contactors, blower motors and clogged condensate lines start showing up.
  • 2020s homes (66). Most of these systems are young enough that a failed part may still be under the manufacturer's parts warranty, especially if the equipment was registered. Labor usually isn't included. Find the model and serial number on the outdoor unit before you call so we can check.

County records list the heat type as forced air and show 170 homes with no basement and 8 with a full basement. They don't say whether a house sits on a crawlspace or a slab, so we won't guess at duct locations street by street.

In ZIP 27571, most homes heat with gas

The Census Bureau's 2024 American Community Survey (5-year) estimates that 65% of occupied homes in ZIP 27571 heat with utility gas, 30% with electricity and about 4.5% with propane. Inside Rolesville town limits the gas share is about 68%. In Knightdale's ZIP 27545 the numbers are flipped: 68% of homes there heat with electricity.

For a Granite Falls homeowner, that usually means a gas furnace indoors paired with a central air conditioner outside. Winter no-heat calls on furnaces like these tend to come down to the hot-surface igniter, the flame sensor, the draft inducer motor or its pressure switch. A heating tune-up should include checking the flue and the burners, not just changing the filter. The Census numbers are an area average, so look before you assume. A metal flue pipe through the roof means gas. An outdoor unit that runs in January with no flue means a heat pump. Gas service is from Enbridge Gas North Carolina (the former PSNC, 877-776-2427). We haven't confirmed which streets have gas lines.

Duke or Wake Electric? Check your bill

The Town of Rolesville says electric service in town is split east and west between Duke Energy Progress and Wake Electric Membership Corporation. The town doesn't publish where the line falls, and we haven't confirmed which side Granite Falls is on. Your power bill is the quick answer. It matters when the power goes out (report the outage to the right company, Duke at 800-452-2777 or Wake Electric at 919-863-6300) and when you compare utility efficiency programs before replacing a system.

Who signs off on HVAC work in Granite Falls

Granite Falls is inside Rolesville town limits. Under the town's permit process, applications go through the Wake County Permit Portal: Rolesville Planning reviews zoning first, then Wake County Permits & Inspections reviews the permit and does the inspections (Wake inspections line: 919-856-6222). Wake County's FAQ says a mechanical permit is required to install, replace, relocate or do general repair on any heating, air-conditioning or venting system. When we replace equipment here, the permit is part of the job.

What we do here: furnace and heating repair, AC repair and furnace replacement when a repair stops making sense.
